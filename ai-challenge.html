A QUICK CHALLENGE / FOUR QUESTIONS

It’s all AI. Or is it?

It’s easy to call an automated task AI. In a real workflow, knowing the difference can save you the cost of building and maintaining a model when a rule will do. Where AI can add value, it helps you decide what to test, who should review the output and what the system can do on its own.

Take a quick break with me. Four tasks have landed in your hiring inbox, and AI has been suggested for all of them. A calendar rule might like a word.

1. Send an interview reminder

Send a reminder 24 hours before each confirmed interview.

Compare with the design recommendation

Rules-based automation. The trigger and action are already defined. Use a calendar rule and test what happens when an interview is moved or cancelled.

2. Forecast interviewer capacity

Estimate how many interviewer hours the hiring team will need next month using past scheduling data.

Compare with the design recommendation

Predictive ML. Try predictive ML if there is enough relevant history. Test it on more recent months and use it only if it improves on a simple forecast.

3. Draft a candidate email

Turn approved interview logistics into a clear, friendly email to the candidate.

Compare with the design recommendation

Generative AI. Use generative AI to draft the message. Have the coordinator check the dates, location and instructions before sending.

4. Recover a disrupted schedule

An interviewer cancels. Finding a replacement may require checking calendars, panel requirements and candidate availability. Each result determines the next check.

Compare with the design recommendation

Agentic AI. A bounded agent could choose which read-only checks to run and propose options. Use a fixed workflow if the steps are predictable, and have the coordinator approve any booking changes.

It helps me ask what goes in, what the model does, what comes out and who relies on it. That gives me a starting point for deciding what to test and where people need to stay involved.
